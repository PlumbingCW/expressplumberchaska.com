Failed Tank Tonight: Limit the Damage First

Most failed tanks seep before they burst, which gives you time to act. Close the cold water valve on top of the heater, turn off the power or gas, and open a hot tap upstairs to relieve pressure.

Move stored items off the floor and keep children and pets out of the area. A wet vacuum handles slow seepage, and our plumber can take over from there.

Signs It Is Time to Replace, Not Repair

Water at the tank base

If water comes from under the jacket and not from a fitting, the steel tank has failed.

Age past ten to twelve years

Even without a leak, an older tank has used up its anode rod and is a flood waiting to happen.

Rust in hot water

Brown water from hot taps only suggests the inside of the tank is corroding.

Repeated repairs

Two repairs in a year on an aging heater usually costs more than a new unit.

Choosing What Goes In: Tank, Heat Pump or Tankless

A standard tank is the lowest upfront cost and easiest swap. A heat pump water heater costs more to buy and uses much less electricity, but needs room and warm air around it. A tankless unit saves floor space and gives continuous hot water, though it needs the right gas line, venting and a regular descale in hard water.

We size the replacement to your household and tell you honestly when the cheapest option is also the right one. If you are leaning toward tankless, read our tankless water heater page.

Water Heater Replacement Cost in Chaska

Typical US installed prices for a standard tank run from about $1,200 to $3,500. Tankless and heat pump units usually run higher. We give a firm price before the old heater comes out. These items change it:

Tank size and efficiencyA 50 gallon high-efficiency unit costs more than a 40 gallon standard model.
VentingSwitching from an atmospheric vent to a power vent or sealed vent adds parts and labor.
Code upgradesExpansion tanks, new shutoff valves, pan drains and strapping where required.
Old unit removal and permitPermit fees and removal of the old unit are part of the visit and listed in the price.

Replacement after a sudden tank burst may be covered by homeowners insurance. A worn-out tank usually is not.

Permits and Inspection in Chaska

The City of Chaska requires a plumbing permit for installation, alteration or repair of plumbing, including water heater and water softener replacement. Homeowners can pull a permit for repairs in their own owner-occupied primary residence, and licensed Minnesota contractors handle other work.

We pull the permit for the jobs we do and schedule the inspection through the City. Inspections can be reached at 952-448-9395 if you want to confirm a date.
