Garbage Disposal Leaking From the Bottom: A Chaska, MN Homeowner Guide

A puddle under the sink and a wet cabinet floor are easy to ignore for a day, and costly to ignore for a month. When water drips from the bottom of a garbage disposal, the cause is usually inside the unit, and the repair is not a tightened bolt.

This guide explains where disposals leak, why a bottom leak usually means the end of the unit and how to stop the water until a plumber can help.

Stop the Water First

1

Turn off the power

Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit or turn off its breaker.

2

Close the shutoff valves

Turn the angle stops under the sink clockwise.

3

Put a bucket under the leak

And dry the cabinet floor.

4

Photograph the leak

It helps with a warranty or insurance claim.

Dry the unit and run water to find the leak. Place dry paper towels under each joint and watch which one gets wet first. A leak from the very bottom means water is escaping through the grinding chamber or the casing.

A bottom leak usually means an internal seal has worn out or the housing has cracked, often from corrosion or from the vibration of years of use.

Why a Bottom Leak Usually Means Replacement

Most disposals are sealed units, and the motor sits right below the grinding chamber. When the seal fails, water reaches the motor and bearings, which accelerates corrosion and risks an electrical short. Repairing the seal rarely holds, and the parts often cost nearly as much as a new unit.

If the unit is under warranty, check the label for the model and manufacturing date and contact the manufacturer before throwing it out.

The Parts of a Disposal and How Each One Leaks

A disposal has a mounting assembly at the sink, a grinding chamber with a spinning plate, a motor below and two outlets, one for the sink drain and one for the dishwasher. Seals at the top and between sections keep water in the chamber.

Water that has leaked around the motor can corrode the housing from the inside. By the time you see a drip at the bottom, the damage has already been under way for a while.

Troubleshooting Checklist Before You Call

1

Dry everything

Wipe the unit, the flange and the hoses so you can see where fresh water appears.

2

Fill the sink and release

Fill the sink basin and let it drain through the disposal. A leak that appears now is at the top or the drain.

3

Run water with the unit on

A leak that appears while grinding points to the housing or a lower seal.

4

Run the dishwasher

If the leak appears only then, check the dishwasher hose and its inlet.

What a Replacement Visit Includes

We disconnect the drain and dishwasher hose, release the old unit from its mounting ring and lower it out of the cabinet. We clean the old putty from the sink flange, set the new mounting assembly and hang the new unit.

Then we reconnect the discharge, knock out the dishwasher plug if there is one, wire the unit and test it with water running. The visit usually takes one to two hours.

DIY or Plumber

Replacing a disposal is a reasonable weekend job for a confident owner. The risks are electrical connections, a misaligned drain and a missed dishwasher plug that backs up the dishwasher.

A plumber is the better call if the sink drain is corroded, the wiring is unclear or the cabinet floor is already rotted.

Protecting the Cabinet While You Wait

Line the cabinet floor with a towel or a shallow tray, and keep the disposal unplugged. Check the tray each morning. A cabinet floor that stays wet for days can swell and grow mold.

Choosing a Replacement

Motor size

A half horsepower unit suits light use. Three quarters to one horsepower grinds tougher waste.

Mounting system

Quick-lock mounts replace old ones faster, but not every sink fits every mount.

Noise

Insulated units run quieter.

Septic compatibility

Some models are designed for septic use, though disposals load a septic tank quickly.

What to Do After the Swap

Run cold water while the disposal grinds and for 15 seconds after. Avoid grease, coffee grounds in quantity, fibrous vegetables and bones. Never use chemical drain cleaner in a disposal.

Check the cabinet again after a day and a week to make sure every joint is dry.

We replace disposals in Chaska, Victoria and Shakopee. If the sink drain slows after a swap, drain cleaning clears the line beyond the unit, and a leaking faucet above is a faucet repair.

Water Damage Under the Sink and Chaska Water

Chaska's drinking water is hard, at 26 grains per gallon in the City's 2025 report, and mineral scale can speed corrosion of fittings and seals under a sink. A leak should be fixed promptly, since the cabinet floor soaks up water faster than most owners expect.

The City requires a plumbing permit for installation, alteration, repair or addition of plumbing, including fixture replacement, and we pull permits for work we do when required.

The disposal connection follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) on which Minnesota's plumbing code is based, including the trap and the dishwasher high loop or air gap.
