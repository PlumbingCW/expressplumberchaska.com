Toilet Keeps Clogging After You Flush? A Chaska, MN Homeowner Guide

A toilet that clogs once is a nuisance. A toilet that clogs every few days is telling you something. The cause may be as simple as too much paper, or as serious as a blockage in the main drain.

This guide explains why toilets clog repeatedly, how to work out whether it is the toilet or the line, what you can fix yourself and when it is time to call.

Start by Deciding: Toilet or Line

Only this toilet clogs

The problem is the toilet itself, its trap or the branch drain right behind it.

Other fixtures are slow too

The blockage is in the shared branch or the main line.

Floor drain or tub gurgles when you flush

Air and water are being forced through a partial main line blockage.

Water rises in the tub when you flush

A clear sign of a main line problem. Stop using water.

Test other fixtures. Run the sink and tub while the toilet is clear. If everything drains normally, the toilet is the suspect. If several fixtures run slow, skip to the main line section below.

What You Can Fix Yourself

1

Use the right plunger

A flange plunger seals the toilet drain better than a flat cup.

2

Use a toilet auger

It reaches past the trap where a plunger cannot.

3

Check the tank water level

The water should be about an inch below the overflow tube.

4

Clean the rim jets

Mineral deposits block the small holes under the rim.

5

Use less paper per flush

Flush twice if needed.

How a Toilet Actually Flushes

When you flush, water from the tank rushes into the bowl through rim holes and a larger siphon jet at the bottom. The surge fills the trap, starts a siphon and pulls the contents down the drain. If anything weakens that surge, such as low tank water, blocked jets or a partial trap blockage, the siphon fails and the bowl does not clear.

That is why a toilet can clog even with a perfectly clear line.

Check the Flange and Wax Ring

If the toilet rocks, leaks at the base or smells of sewer gas, the flange or wax ring may have failed. A poor seal lets air escape and weakens the flush. A raised floor from a tile remodel can leave the flange too low.

A plumber can reset the toilet with a new wax ring and a flange extender to fix both.

Children and Foreign Objects

Toys, wipes, cotton balls and bath items are frequent culprits in homes with kids. A toilet that suddenly won't flush may have an object stuck in the trap.

A toilet auger often retrieves it. If the object is hard or stuck, the toilet may need to be pulled to clear it.

What Belongs in the Bowl and What Does Not

Toilet paper and human waste are the only things a toilet is built to carry. Wipes labeled flushable still clump in the trap and collect on rough pipe walls.

Keep a small covered trash can in the bathroom for wipes, cotton and dental floss. Showing children where it is stops many repeat clogs.

Older Toilets, Newer Toilets

Toilets made in the early 1990s used 3.5 gallons per flush or more, and many were replaced by 1.6 gallon models that performed poorly at first. Modern 1.28 gallon toilets flush much better, but only if the trap and drain are clear.

A toilet that has clogged since the day it was installed may have a design flaw or a bad install. A toilet that started clogging after years of service may have a developing line problem.

When It Is the Line

A main line partially blocked by roots, grease or a belly will make toilets clog at random. A cable through the main line clears it, and a camera shows what caused it.

If the toilet clogs again after a clean main line, the toilet itself or its flange may be at fault.

What Not to Flush

Flushable wipes are one of the most common causes of repeated clogs. They do not break down like toilet paper. Cotton swabs, dental floss, feminine products, paper towels and diapers do not belong in a toilet either.

If you use wipes, put them in the trash. It is the cheapest plumbing fix there is.

We fix clogging toilets in Chaska, Shakopee and Waconia. For a stubborn main line, sewer line cleaning is the next step, and a worn toilet may be best handled by toilet repair or replacement.

Chaska Sewer Basics Behind Your Toilet

The sewer service line from your property to the City of Chaska's main is the responsibility of the property owner. The City main flows to a Metropolitan Council lift station and on to the Blue Lake Wastewater Treatment Facility in Shakopee.

If several neighbors report backups at once, call the Water and Sewer Department at 952-448-4335. Replacing a toilet requires a plumbing permit in Chaska, which homeowners may pull for their own primary residence.

Replacement toilets must meet current flush-volume standards, and installation follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Minnesota's code is based on.
