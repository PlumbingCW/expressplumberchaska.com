Kitchen Sink Plumbing Installation in Chaska, MN: A Homeowner Guide

A new kitchen sink looks like a simple swap until the old drain does not line up, the shutoff valves seize and the disposal needs a new mount. Good planning keeps a one-day job from turning into a three-day one.

This guide covers what the plumbing under the sink involves, what to decide before the day, what a plumber will check and what Chaska expects for permits.

What Sits Under a Kitchen Sink

Two supply lines bring hot and cold water to the faucet, each with its own shutoff valve. A drain assembly runs from the strainer basket to a trap, then into the wall. A disposal and a dishwasher connection often share that drain.

A vent in the wall lets the drain breathe. Without it, water gurgles and the trap can siphon dry.

Decisions to Make Before the Plumber Arrives

Sink style and depth

A deep or farmhouse sink changes drain height and cabinet space.

Single or double bowl

Double bowls need a continuous waste connection to one trap.

Faucet holes

Match the number of faucet holes to the faucet you plan to use.

Disposal and filter taps

Plan the outlets, air gaps and any filtered water line.

Choose the sink and faucet first. Swapping them later means taking the plumbing apart twice. Have the faucet and sink on site on the day so we can fit and seal everything before the countertop is final.

Sink Materials and What They Mean for the Plumbing

Stainless steel sinks are light and mount in many ways. Cast iron and fireclay sinks are heavy and need cabinet support. Composite and granite sinks need undermount clips and careful sealing. Farmhouse sinks often need the cabinet cut to fit.

Deep sinks hang lower, which can leave less room for the disposal and trap. Measure the cabinet before you buy.

Dishwasher Connections and the High Loop

The dishwasher drain hose must rise to the underside of the countertop before it drops to the disposal or the drain. This high loop stops dirty sink water from running back into the dishwasher.

Some jurisdictions require an air gap instead. We follow the code that applies and explain which one your sink uses.

A Checklist for After the Install

Dry-towel test

Place a dry paper towel under each joint and check it after an hour.

Fill-and-drain test

Fill both bowls and release them at once. A leak at the trap shows under the load.

Cabinet check after a week

Slow weeps appear after a few days of use.

Shutoff valve test

Close and open each valve to confirm it turns freely.

Planning for Countertops

Undermount sinks are set after the countertop is cut, and the plumber usually connects the drain and faucet after the counter is installed. Coordinate the date with your countertop installer so the work flows in the right order.

If you are replacing a drop-in sink with an undermount, the cutout changes, so the countertop must be modified or replaced.

Plan the Cabinet Before You Buy the Sink

Measure the inside width of the base cabinet and the depth from front to back. A sink that is wider than the cabinet allows will not fit no matter how well it is installed.

Check the cutout template that comes in the box and compare it to the countertop. If the counter is stone or solid surface, the cutout is best done before the sink arrives.

We close the supply valves and remove items from under the sink.

Disconnect supply lines, the trap and any disposal.

Seal the sink to the counter and mount the faucet.

4

Connect drain and supply

5

Run water, fill the sink, check every joint with a dry paper towel.

What It Costs

Typical US prices for replacing a kitchen sink and faucet commonly run from about $300 to $900 for labor, plus fixtures. Adding a disposal, moving the drain or replacing old shutoff valves adds to it.

The biggest cost surprise is hidden damage. A cabinet floor that has been wet for years may need repair before the new sink goes in.

Common Mistakes

Using old rubber washers in new fittings, over-tightening plastic nuts, skipping the dishwasher high loop and reusing a corroded trap are the usual causes of a new sink leak. A slow drip under a sink soaks the cabinet before anyone notices.

Check the cabinet after the first day and again after a week with a dry towel under each joint.

We serve Chaska, Waconia and Shakopee. A failing disposal is handled by garbage disposal repair, and a faucet that drips is a faucet repair.

Permits for Fixture Work in Chaska

The City of Chaska requires a plumbing permit for installation, alteration, repair or addition of plumbing, including fixture replacement. A homeowner may pull a permit for work in an owner-occupied primary residence, and the City uses the SmartGov portal for permits.

Inspections can be reached at 952-448-9395 or inspections@chaskamn.gov. We pull permits for the work we do when they are required.

The trap, vent and drain slope under a kitchen sink follow the Minnesota Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
