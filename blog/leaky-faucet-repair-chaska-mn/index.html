Leaky Sink Faucet Repair for Chaska, MN Homes: Step-by-Step

A dripping faucet wastes water and costs money, and the sound wears on everyone in the house. Most leaky faucets can be fixed with a few inexpensive parts, if you know what type of faucet you have and where the water is coming from.

This guide walks through the four main faucet types, how to find the cause of the leak, how a repair works and when to replace instead.

Know Your Faucet Type

TypeHow to tellUsual failed part
CompressionTwo handles that you tighten downRubber washer and seat
BallSingle handle over a rounded capSprings and seals
CartridgeSingle or double handle, smooth motionCartridge or O-rings
Ceramic discSingle handle, very smooth, wide bodyDisc cylinder and seals

The type matters because the parts are not interchangeable. If you are unsure, remove the handle and look inside, or take a photo to a plumber or supplier.

Where Is the Water Coming From

Drips from the spout

Worn washer, seat or cartridge. The most common leak.

Leaks at the handle

Worn O-ring or packing nut.

Leaks at the base of the faucet

Failed seal or loose mounting.

Leaks under the sink

Supply line, shutoff valve or drain connection.

How a Basic Repair Goes

1

Shut off the water

Close the shutoff valves under the sink. Open the faucet to drain it.

2

Plug the drain

A cloth in the drain stops small parts from falling in.

3

Remove the handle

Pry off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle.

4

Replace the worn part

Take the old cartridge, washer or O-ring to match it exactly.

5

Reassemble and test

Turn the water on slowly and check for drips.

Tools and Parts You Will Need

A basic repair needs an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver set, an Allen key set for set screws and replacement parts. Take the old cartridge or washer with you when you buy the replacement, since there are many sizes.

Have plumber's grease on hand for O-rings and a small container to hold screws. Lay a towel over the sink to protect the finish.

Mistakes That Cause a Second Leak

Overtightening

Cranking a packing nut or cartridge nut can crack the body.

Reusing a worn O-ring

A new cartridge with an old seal still leaks.

Skipping the valve seat

A pitted seat chews up a new washer in weeks.

Mixing parts

Parts from another brand may seem to fit and then leak.

Tub, Shower and Outdoor Faucets

A shower that drips from the spout after shutting off needs a new cartridge or stem, usually reached through the handle and escutcheon. A tub spout that leaks at the wall may have a failed seal.

Outdoor hose bibs split when a hose is left connected in freezing weather. If water drips inside the wall after the bib thaws, call a plumber before the stain spreads.

Tools and Parts to Gather First

Most faucet repairs need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver, a hex key set and a small towel for the drain. A cartridge or washer kit that matches the brand is cheaper than guessing.

Close the shutoff valves under the sink before you start. Plug the drain so a small screw does not disappear into the pipe, then open the faucet to release the pressure.

If the faucet is more than fifteen years old or the finish is pitted, a new faucet often costs little more than the repair parts. A plumber can tell you which makes sense once the handle is off.

When to Replace Instead of Repair

If the faucet is pitted or corroded, if parts are discontinued or if you have already replaced the cartridge more than once, a new faucet is the better buy. A new faucet also gives you a chance to upgrade to a model with a pull-down sprayer or a more durable finish.

Replacement is also the answer if the leak is from the faucet body or the base, since those seals are rarely replaceable.

Why Hard Water Shortens Faucet Life

Minerals in hard water build up inside the cartridge, harden the seals and scratch the ceramic surfaces. Clean aerators every few months by soaking them in vinegar, and expect to replace cartridges more often than in soft water areas.

A water softener cuts that wear at the source.

The Cost of Ignoring a Drip

A faucet dripping once per second wastes thousands of gallons a year. That shows up on the water and sewer bill. The City's meter test and its four-day consumption alert are good ways to confirm a hidden leak if the drip is not obvious.

A repair that costs under $250 is a bargain next to months of waste.

Hard Water Facts for Chaska Faucets

The City of Chaska's 2025 drinking water report lists the water at 26 grains per gallon and says a water softener is very important to improving the aesthetics of the water. At that hardness, white scale on aerators and cartridges is normal.

A plumbing permit is required in Chaska for repair or replacement of fixtures, although homeowners may pull one for their own primary residence.
