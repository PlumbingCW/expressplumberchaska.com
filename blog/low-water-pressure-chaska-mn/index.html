Low Water Pressure in Chaska, MN Homes: Causes and Fixes

Weak water pressure can mean a clogged shower head, a failing pressure regulator, a hidden leak or a problem outside the house. The trick is working out whether the problem is at one fixture, throughout the house, or from the street.

This guide walks through the checks that find the cause, from the simplest to the more involved, and explains when a plumber is worth calling.

One Fixture or the Whole House

Only one faucet or shower

A clogged aerator, shower head or cartridge.

Only hot water

A water heater issue, sediment or a partly closed valve.

Only cold water

A cold line restriction or a partly closed valve.

Every tap in the house

The main, the regulator, the service line or the supply.

Quick Checks for a Single Fixture

1

Unscrew the aerator

Soak it in vinegar and brush off the scale.

2

Check the shower head

Mineral deposits plug the nozzles. A vinegar soak helps.

3

Check the angle stop

Make sure the valve under the sink is fully open.

4

Check the cartridge

A worn or clogged cartridge reduces flow.

If cleaning the aerator brings the flow back, you are done. If it does not, the supply line may be kinked, or the valve behind it is failing.

How to Read a Pressure Gauge

A simple gauge screws onto an outdoor faucet. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, open the faucet and read the needle. Most homes run between 40 and 60 psi. Below 30 is noticeably weak, and above 80 stresses fixtures and appliances.

Test at different times of day. A reading that drops in the evening points to neighborhood demand, while a steadily low reading points to a problem at your home.

Pressure Reducing Valves Explained

A pressure reducing valve is a bell-shaped device near where the main enters the house. It lowers incoming street pressure to a safe level. Over years, its internal parts wear and the valve may drift low, or it may stick open and let high pressure through.

A plumber can test it with gauges before and after the valve. Replacing one is a common and fairly quick repair.

Water Heaters, Softeners and Filters

A water heater clogged with sediment can reduce hot water flow. A softener or filter with a plugged cartridge or resin bed can slow the whole house. Open the bypass on a softener for a minute to see if pressure returns.

If it does, the device needs service. If not, the problem is elsewhere.

When Low Pressure Points to a Hidden Leak

A sudden drop in pressure with a spinning meter and a damp spot in the yard or basement often means a break. A wet patch in the lawn or a constantly running sound in the walls are clues.

In that case, close the main and call. A small leak wastes water and can undermine soil, and a large one can flood the house.

A Ten-Minute Test to Narrow Down the Cause

Open a cold tap in the kitchen, then the bathroom and then an outdoor spigot. If pressure is low everywhere, the cause is near the main or the pressure regulator.

If only one fixture is weak, look at that faucet's aerator or shutoff valve. Pressure that drops only on the hot side points to the water heater or its piping.

When Low Pressure Is Not Your Plumbing

Pressure can dip when the city is flushing hydrants or repairing a main nearby, and it usually returns within hours. For a billing or main question, the City of Chaska Water and Sewer line at 952-448-4335 can tell you whether work is under way.

If neighbors have the same problem and the City confirms nothing is going on, call us. A failing pressure regulator on the line into the house is a common cause and is quick to test.

Common Causes Behind the Wall and Outside

A failing pressure reducing valve can drop pressure far below what the street provides. A leaking service line between the meter and the house can reduce pressure and soak the yard. Old galvanized pipe closes down with rust until it barely carries water.

If the pressure fell suddenly, suspect a valve, a leak or a municipal work in the area. If it fell slowly over years, suspect pipe corrosion or scale.

When to Call: The Street or the House

If your gauge shows low pressure and neighbors have the same trouble, the cause may be at the City main, a fire flow event or work on the system. Call the utility to ask.

If only your home is affected and the gauge is low at the hose bib, the problem is between the meter and your house.

We help homes in Chaska, Chanhassen and Victoria. A hidden leak is a leak detection job, and an old failing line may call for repiping or water line repair.

What Chaska Water Brings to Your Pipes

The City of Chaska draws water from seven wells between 333 and 817 feet deep, removes iron and manganese and adds fluoride. The water is hard, at 26 grains per gallon in the 2025 report, so scale in aerators, shower heads and older pipes is a common reason for weak flow.

The Water and Sewer Department is at 952-448-4335 for questions about pressure at your address. Before digging for a line repair, call Gopher State One Call at 651-454-0002.

Plumbing codes based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which includes Minnesota's, treat 80 psi as the upper limit at fixtures, which is why a pressure reducing valve is common.
