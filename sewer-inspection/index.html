Sewer Inspection in Chaska, MN

A sewer camera inspection shows what is inside the pipe without digging: roots, cracks, offsets, bellies, grease and scale. It is the single best way to know whether the line needs cleaning, lining or replacement.

We run sewer inspections for Chaska homebuyers, owners with repeat clogs and anyone planning a dig, and we give you the recorded video and a plain-language summary of what we saw.

What a Sewer Camera Inspection Shows

A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels from a cleanout or fixture through the line to the street connection. A transmitter in the head lets us locate the pipe from above, so we can mark depth and position on the ground.

You see the same picture we do, in real time, and you receive the recording afterward.

Roots

Fine hairs at joints or thick masses that fill the pipe.

Cracks and breaks

Hairline cracks, missing sections or offset joints that leak or catch debris.

Bellies

Low spots where water and solids settle and clog repeatedly.

Grease and scale

Coatings that narrow the pipe and point to jetting.

Pipe material

Clay, cast iron, Orangeburg, PVC. Each fails in its own way.

When an Inspection Is Worth It

1

Before you buy

A pre-purchase inspection of the sewer lateral can reveal a problem before closing, when you still have leverage.

2

After a repeat clog

If a line has backed up twice, the camera finds the cause.

3

Before a dig

Locating the pipe and its depth helps plan landscaping, a deck or a new line.

4

After a repair

A post-repair pass confirms the work, and the recording is your proof.

Sewer Inspection Cost

A camera inspection typically runs from about $150 to $400 for a straightforward residential line in typical US pricing, often more if cleaning is needed first. The price depends on:

AccessA clean, accessible cleanout makes the pass quick.
Pre-cleaningIf the line is blocked, we clear it before the camera can pass.
LengthA long lateral with several bends takes longer.
LocatingMarking the line's path and depth above ground is added when needed.

Inspection fees are not covered by insurance, but a recorded finding can support a claim for sudden damage or a negotiation during a home purchase.

Home Age and Sewer Lines in Chaska

Chaska has grown by roughly 100 to 300 homes a year in recent years, so the town has a mix of older downtown housing and newer subdivisions. Older sewer laterals commonly use clay or cast iron, while newer ones use PVC, and the inspection shows which you have.

The service line from your property to the City main is the owner's responsibility, so you want to know its condition. Wastewater from the city ends at the Blue Lake Wastewater Treatment Facility in Shakopee.
