Emergency Hydro Jetting in Chaska, MN

If a drain clogs again within weeks of being snaked, the cable probably punched a hole through the blockage and left the rest on the pipe wall. Stop pouring grease or chemical cleaner down it and call us before the next backup.

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream to scour grease, scale, sludge and root hairs off the pipe wall, restoring the full diameter of the line in Chaska homes, restaurants and small commercial buildings.

Hydro Jetting Versus Snaking: What Is the Difference

A cable cuts a hole through a clog. A jet scours the whole pipe wall and washes debris down the line. For a one-time hair clog, a cable is the right tool. For recurring grease, scale or root hair, a jet cleans the full pipe.

We use a camera first on any line that has backed up more than once, so we know whether the pipe is sound enough to jet and what we are clearing.

What Jetting Clears

Grease and soap film

Kitchen and laundry lines build a coat of grease that narrows the pipe over months.

Mineral scale

Hard water leaves deposits in older iron and steel drain lines.

Root hairs

Fine roots enter at joints and catch paper. A jet cuts them back, though it does not stop them returning.

Sludge in horizontal runs

Slow flowing basement lines collect settled solids that a cable pushes around.

Is Your Pipe Safe to Jet?

Jetting is not for every pipe. Cracked clay, fragile cast iron with deep corrosion and pipes that have collapsed or have offset joints can be damaged by high pressure. A camera shows the condition before we pressure up.

If the camera shows damage, we explain the options and recommend sewer line repair instead of jetting. We would rather tell you no than break a pipe.

Hydro Jetting Cost

Hydro jetting typically costs more than a cable visit, with typical US prices from roughly $350 to $800 or more depending on line length and access. The camera inspection is priced with it. The price depends on:

Line length and sizeA 4 inch branch is quicker than a 6 inch main run to the street.
Access pointA cleanout makes it easy. A toilet pull adds time.
Amount of buildupHeavy grease or root masses need more passes.
Camera reviewA recorded camera pass before and after adds time and gives you proof.

Jetting for maintenance is not an insurance claim. A sewer backup rider, if you have one, may apply to damage from a sudden backup.

Roots, Hard Water and Older Neighborhoods in Chaska

Chaska's water is reported at 26 grains per gallon in the City's 2025 drinking water report, which means scale can build in older iron drain lines. Mature trees along older streets, also send roots into clay and cast iron joints.

The sewer service line from the property to the City main is the owner's responsibility, so jetting your side is up to you, and the City main belongs to the City.

Jetting is done through accessible cleanouts, which the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for on drain lines, and we cannot safely jet a line that has no access point.
