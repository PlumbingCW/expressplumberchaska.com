A Growing City and What It Means for Its Plumbing

Victoria's population reached 10,546 in the 2020 census, up 43.6 percent from 2010, with about 3,719 housing units. Newer homes mean newer systems, but also the first water heater swaps and the first sump pump failures.

The city is also a place of lakes, with 13 of them and the 1,800-acre Carver Park Reserve nearby. Lake Bavaria and the other lakes raise the groundwater that new basements sit against.

How Your Address Is Served

We do not state a water hardness for Victoria because we have not confirmed it from a City source. The simplest test is to check your faucet aerator for white scale, or ask Victoria's Utility Billing office at 952-443-4229 how your address is served.

The City's history is visible in St. Victoria Church, dating to 1857, and older farmsteads on the edges of town may use private wells and septic systems.

Plumbing in Victoria follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), whether the home uses city utilities or a private well and septic system.

Lake-Area and Seasonal Homes

A home on or near one of Victoria's lakes sees plumbing stress that a typical subdivision does not. Seasonal use leaves water sitting in lines, vacant winters risk freezing, and a high water table loads the sump pit year-round.

If you open or close a seasonal property, we can drain the lines, protect the water heater and test the sump, so spring does not start with a flood.

What We Bring to a Victoria Call

Our trucks carry common water heater parts, sump pump components, fittings, drain cables and a leak locating kit. That lets us fix many problems on the first visit, and when we need a part, we tell you before we leave.

For a failed heater beyond repair, our water heater installation team can plan the replacement, including permits.
