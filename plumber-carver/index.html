Carver: River Town, Flood History and Old Pipes

Carver was platted in 1857, became a village in 1877 and served as a steamboat terminal on the Minnesota River. Its historic district is on the National Register of Historic Places, listed in 1980 with 87 buildings, so homes in that part of town may still carry their original drain lines.

The town flooded badly in April 1965, and a floodwall followed under the Flood Control Act of 1965. Older buildings often have cast iron drains and galvanized supply lines, and the high water table near the river is why sumps and backups matter.

Repairs in Carver follow the Minnesota Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including its rules for backwater valves where one is installed.

Backwater Valves and Floor Drains

A backwater valve is a one-way valve on the sewer line that closes when sewage tries to flow back into the house. In low-lying areas near a river it can prevent the worst kind of basement backup.

It needs a proper cleanout and must be installed to code with a permit. We can inspect your line, show you whether a valve fits and explain the maintenance it needs.
