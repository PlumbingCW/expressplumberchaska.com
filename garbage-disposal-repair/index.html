What to Do About a Leak or Flood Under the Sink

Turn off the disposal switch and unplug the unit. Close the shutoff valves and place a bucket under the leak. Look at where the water appears: from the top flange, the side drain hose or the bottom housing.

A leak from the bottom of the unit means the housing has failed and the disposal must be replaced. Leaks from the flange or hose are usually repairable.

Common Disposal Problems

Hums, will not spin

A jam. A hex key at the bottom turns the flywheel and frees it.

Dead, no hum

The reset button on the bottom has tripped or the unit has lost power.

Slow draining sink

The clog is often past the disposal in the drain line.

Smells bad

Food residue in the grind chamber and baffle. A clean-out helps.

Grinds loudly

Foreign objects or worn parts inside.

Disposal on the blink?

Call and tell us if it hums, leaks or is dead. We will tell you whether it sounds like a repair or a replacement.

Garbage Disposal Repair and Replacement

A jam or reset commonly costs about $100 to $200 in typical US pricing. A new disposal installed commonly runs from about $250 to $600, depending on the motor size. What drives it:

Motor sizeA half horsepower unit costs less than a one horsepower model.
ElectricalA new outlet or switch adds cost.
Drain conditionA clogged line below the unit is a separate service.
Existing mountMatching the old mount saves time, a new style needs adaptation.

Disposal repair is rarely an insurance claim. Water damage from a sudden leak under the sink may be.

What Not to Put Down a Disposal

Grease, coffee grounds in quantity, potato peels, fibrous vegetables like celery and rice all cause trouble. Bones and fruit pits can jam the blades. Run cold water while it runs and for 15 seconds after.

If the kitchen sink keeps backing up despite a working disposal, the line beyond it needs drain cleaning.

Disposals, Septic and Sewer in Chaska

Chaska properties served by the City sewer send wastewater to a Metropolitan Council lift station and on to the Blue Lake treatment plant, so a disposal is acceptable on City sewer. Properties on a private septic system need more caution, since disposal solids load a tank quickly.

Fixture and appliance replacement requires a plumbing permit in the City, and we handle it for the jobs we do.

The disposal's connection to the sink drain follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) on which Minnesota's plumbing code is based, including the trap and the dishwasher high loop or air gap.
