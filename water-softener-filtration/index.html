What 26 Grains Per Gallon Means for Your Home

Hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. At 26 grains per gallon, a household can see crusty fixtures, spotty dishes, dry skin and soap that will not lather. Inside the pipes, scale narrows lines and coats heating elements, which wastes energy.

The City report states that a water softener is very important to improving the water's aesthetics. Iron and manganese are removed at the City plant, so the main job is hardness.

How We Size a Softener for Your Household

Sizing is simple arithmetic. Multiply the number of people by about 75 gallons of daily use, then by the water hardness in grains. Four people using 75 gallons each at 26 grains per gallon need to remove about 7,800 grains a day, or roughly 55,000 grains between weekly regenerations.

A unit that is too small regenerates too often and wastes salt and water. A unit that is far too large can let water sit and go stale. We size so that regeneration happens about once a week.

Salt, Water and Drain Needs

A softener needs a nearby drain for its regeneration discharge, a power outlet for the controller and space for the brine tank. The brine drains to the house sewer, and the install includes an air gap so wastewater cannot siphon back.

We also fit a bypass valve, so you can shut off softened water to the outdoor taps, since lawns and gardens do not need it.

Permits and Reverse Osmosis in Chaska

The City of Chaska requires a plumbing permit for water softener installation or replacement. Reverse osmosis systems are exempt from the permit requirement.

Seven City wells draw from several aquifers at depths of 333 to 817 feet. Because hard water reaches your tap, a softener on the home's main water line protects everything downstream. See tankless water heater for how scale affects heaters.

Softener drains need an air gap under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Minnesota's plumbing code follows, so brine discharge cannot siphon back into the supply.
