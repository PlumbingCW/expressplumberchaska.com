What to Do When the Sump Pit Fills and the Pump Will Not Run

Check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker is on. Lift the float by hand. If the pump starts, the switch is stuck or tangled. If it does not, the motor has failed.

While you wait, a wet vacuum and a bucket can buy time. Move boxes and electronics off the floor, and keep away from outlets near standing water.

Pump runs nonstop

A stuck float, a failed check valve or high water table.

Pump hums but no water moves

Clogged impeller or a frozen or blocked discharge line.

Loud grinding or rattling

Worn bearings or debris.

No float movement

Switch tangled or stuck.

Pump is more than ten years old

Even working pumps are near the end of their life.

Battery Backup: The Part That Saves the Basement

Storms that overload the grid also take out the power your pump needs. A battery backup pump keeps the pit emptied for hours without power. It is a separate pump that sits beside the main one.

We test the main pump, the float and the backup battery on every visit, and we replace weak batteries before they fail.

Test Your Pump Each Spring

1

Pour in water

Slowly fill the pit with a bucket of water until the float rises.

2

Watch the cycle

The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off without chattering.

3

Check the discharge

Walk outside and confirm water flows from the end of the pipe and away from the foundation.

4

Test the backup

Unplug the main pump and see whether the battery unit starts.

Sump Pump Repair and Replacement Cost

A repair such as a float switch commonly runs from about $150 to $400 in typical US pricing, and a new pump installed from about $500 to $1,200, with battery backup extra. What changes it:

Pump sizeHorsepower and flow rate match the pit and water table.
Backup systemA battery backup adds equipment and labor.
Discharge routeA longer or buried discharge costs more.
Pit conditionA cracked or undersized pit may need work.

Water damage from a pump failure may be excluded unless you carry water backup coverage. Ask your insurer about the rider.

Where Sump Water Can Go in Chaska

The City of Chaska says water from groundwater sump pumps cannot be pumped into the sanitary sewer. Discharge must go outside to the yard or storm drainage, away from the foundation and neighbors.

Chaska sits near the Minnesota River, and spring thaws and heavy storms raise groundwater. A working sump and a backup are standard protection for basements here. If your basement stays wet, our basement waterproofing service addresses the cause.
