Sewage in the Basement: First Steps

Stop all water use. Turn off the furnace and water heater if sewage has reached them, and avoid touching the spill without gloves and boots. Keep children and pets away.

Call us to clear the line and assess the pipe. If this is the first backup, a clearing may restore service, and a camera will show whether a repair is needed.

How We Decide Between Repair, Lining and Replacement

ConditionBest fitWhy
One cracked or offset jointSpot repairDig only where needed
Long run of root intrusion, sound pipePipe liningNo trench, new pipe inside the old
Collapsed or belly in the lineReplace the sectionA liner cannot fix a low spot
Entire line failingFull replacementNew pipe from house to main

Trenchless lining slides a resin-coated liner through the old pipe and cures it into a smooth new surface. It works when the pipe is basically in line. When a section has dropped, we replace it and tell you why.

Whatever the method, you get a written scope and price before work starts. A short sewer inspection first keeps the quote accurate.

Signs Your Sewer Line Is Failing

Several drains back up at once

A main line problem shows in the lowest fixtures first.

Sewer odor in the yard or basement

A cracked pipe leaks gas and sometimes liquid.

Lush patch of grass or sunken ground

A leaking line feeds the soil or washes out the bedding.

Repeated clogs after cleaning

Roots, offsets or a belly keep catching debris.

Sewer Line Repair Cost

Sewer repairs span a wide range. Typical US figures run from about $1,500 for a short spot repair to $10,000 or more for a long replacement or lining. We price after the camera. These factors decide it:

Length and depthA shallow 15 foot repair costs less than a deep 60 foot run.
MethodSpot digging, lining and full replacement carry different labor and material costs.
Surface restorationDriveways, patios and landscaping affect the final scope.
Permits and locatesUtility locates and any City permit are part of the job.

Homeowners policies often exclude gradual pipe failure, but some include service line coverage as an add-on. Check with your insurer. Some utilities and third parties also sell line protection plans.

Who Owns Which Part of the Line in Chaska

The City says the sewer service line from your property to the City's main is the owner's responsibility. Wastewater from the city flows to a Metropolitan Council lift station and then to the Blue Lake Wastewater Treatment Facility in Shakopee.

Before any digging, call Gopher State One Call at 651-454-0002 so buried utilities are marked. If a repair affects the street connection, we coordinate with the City Water and Sewer Department at 952-448-4335.

Sewer repairs use approved pipe and fittings and the slope the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requires, which Minnesota's plumbing code follows, and the City may inspect the work.
