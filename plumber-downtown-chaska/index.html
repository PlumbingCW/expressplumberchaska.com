Plumber in Downtown Chaska, MN

Downtown Chaska sits at the south end of the city, below the bluff, around Chestnut Street, Second Street and Chaska Boulevard. It is the historic core of the city, and it still has many older single-family homes close to Walnut Street and the river.

If you live or work here and have a slow drain, a leak or a damp basement, call us. We will talk through the problem and tell you what the visit involves before work starts.

Older Streets Close to the Walnut Street Historic District

Downtown Chaska grew up around the brickmaking industry that began here in 1857. The Walnut Street Historic District covers about 17 acres roughly bounded by Walnut, Second, Chestnut and Sixth Streets. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on January 4, 1980, and its buildings date from 1860 to 1920.

That history matters to a homeowner for one practical reason. Older buildings have been repaired and added to many times, so the pipes in the ground and inside the walls may not match what the original plans show. A camera look is the only way to know.

Slow drains that keep returning

A drain that clears and slows again may be a partial blockage or a damaged line. See drain cleaning for what we do first.

Water in the basement

Check the floor drain, the sump pit and the wall base. Sump pump repair covers the pump and the discharge.

Sewer smell or gurgling

Gurgling at the toilet or a smell near a floor drain can point to the sewer line. Sewer inspection shows what is in the pipe.

Damp spots or a high water bill

A hidden leak can show up as a bill you cannot explain. Leak detection starts with a meter test.

What the City Says About Housing Downtown

The City of Chaska's 2012 Downtown Master Plan describes the Downtown West neighborhood as exclusively single-family detached homes, with eight homes holding a historic designation between First and Second Streets. The southern part of Downtown East is almost entirely detached single-family homes, including ten historic-designated homes.

The plan also notes that Walnut Street mixes higher-density housing, single-family homes, shops and offices. Near the river, five small blocks east of Chestnut Street are mostly single-family houses, and the levee separates them from the water.

Who Owns Which Part of the Line

In Chaska, the homeowner owns the sewer service line from the house to the city main, and the city owns the main in the street. That split is easy to forget on an older street where the line may run a long way under the yard or the driveway.

If a sewer backup happens, a quick call to the City of Chaska Water and Sewer line at 952-448-4335 can tell you whether the main is the problem. If it is not, the blockage is on your side and we can clear it or inspect it.

Sump pump water and the sewer

The City says sump pump water cannot go into the sanitary sewer. If you are not sure where your pump discharges, we can look at it during a visit and explain what we see.

Plumbing Services We Offer Downtown

Downtown homes tend to need a mix of drain work, sewer work and fixture repairs. Water heaters and shutoff valves also come up, since many homes here have been updated one room at a time.

If you are planning a larger update, ask us about repiping before opening walls, so the work can be done in one visit.
