How Dispatch Works Across the Area

Calls come into one line, and a person decides which plumber is closest and best equipped. We ask for the street, the town and what is happening, then tell you what to shut off and what to avoid while we get there.

We do not promise a fixed arrival time, since distance, traffic and weather vary. We do tell you honestly what to expect when we dispatch, and we call if something changes.

Highways, Lakes and the River

Highways 41 and 212 tie Chaska to Chanhassen and Shakopee, and Highway 5 serves Chanhassen and Waconia. The Minnesota River runs between Chaska and Shakopee, and Highway 41 has been closed at times for river flooding.

When a closure or a storm changes the route, we plan around it and tell you if it affects timing. That is also why we like to hear your street name early in the call.

What Changes From Town to Town

Water, ground and housing differ across the area. Chanhassen reports city water at about 21 grains per gallon and recommends a home softener, while Chaska reports 26 grains. Victoria and Waconia sit among lakes where groundwater runs high, and Carver has a flood history that makes sump pumps and backwater valves worth a look.

Because the problems differ, so does our advice. A water heater in a 1990s Chanhassen subdivision, a sump pit near a Victoria lake and a drain in a historic Carver building each call for a slightly different approach.
