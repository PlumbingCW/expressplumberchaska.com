Chaska Plumbing, Locally

What Makes Plumbing in Chaska Different

Chaska's brickmaking began in 1857, the city grew from a village in 1871 into a chartered city in 1891, and the Jonathan new town concept was introduced in 1966. That layering matters under the floor: older streets, newer subdivisions and a steady stream of fresh construction all share the same City water and sewer system. Work on that system follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

The City has added roughly 100 to 300 homes a year in recent years. That keeps demand high for water heaters, drains and sump pumps, and it is why we see both first-generation fixtures failing and decades-old pipes still in service.

Hard water, 26 grains

The City reports hard water at 26 grains per gallon. Scale shortens water heater life and clogs aerators.
