Leaking Tank or No Hot Water: What to Do While You Wait

A leak from the tank body is almost never repairable, but a leak from a fitting, valve or drain often is. Turn off the heater's power at the breaker or set the gas control to off, then shut the cold supply valve so the tank stops refilling.

If the floor drain is nearby, let the water run there. If not, a wet vacuum and a few towels buy time. Do not touch wiring or the burner area while it is wet.

Common Water Heater Faults We Repair

No hot water at all

On an electric unit this is often a tripped breaker, a failed upper element or a bad thermostat. On gas it is usually a pilot, thermocouple or gas valve fault.

Lukewarm water

A failed lower element, a stuck dip tube or a thermostat set too low. Sediment can also blanket the burner or element.

Rumbling or popping

Hard water scale on the tank bottom traps steam. Flushing helps, but a heavily scaled tank may be near the end of its life.

Water from the relief valve

A weeping valve can mean high pressure or a worn seat. A valve discharging steadily is a safety issue and needs a plumber the same day.

Rust-colored hot water

Corroded anode rod or tank lining. If only hot water is brown, the tank is likely corroding from inside.

Pilot will not stay lit

A worn thermocouple or a draft. A new thermocouple is a small, inexpensive part when it is caught early.

Repair or Replace: How We Decide

A tank that is under about eight years old with a failed part is usually worth repairing. A tank that is leaking from the body, rusting through or more than ten to twelve years old is better replaced, and our water heater replacement service handles that same visit when it makes sense.

We tell you the cost of both paths before any work starts so the decision is yours.

What Water Heater Repairs Cost

Small parts such as a thermocouple, element or thermostat sit at the low end of typical US repair ranges, roughly $150 to $450 with labor. Gas valves, tankless control boards and heat exchangers cost more. You get the price before we begin, and these factors set it:

PartA thermocouple costs far less than a gas valve or a tankless board.
Age of the heaterOn an older unit we explain whether the repair is likely to last.
Fuel and typeGas, electric, heat pump and tankless each need different parts and skills.
AccessA heater in a tight utility closet or crawl space takes longer than one in an open basement.

Ask your insurer whether a sudden leak from a failed appliance is covered. Slow leaks and age-related failures often are not.

Hard Water and Chaska Water Heaters

The City of Chaska's 2025 drinking water report lists the city's water as hard, at 26 grains per gallon, and says a water softener is very important for the water's aesthetics. Hard water leaves scale on heating elements and the tank floor, which is why we see element failures and rumbling tanks here.

Flushing the tank once a year slows scale. A water softener reduces it at the source.

Relief valves and discharge pipes are checked against the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which Minnesota's plumbing code is based on, and we do not cap or bypass a safety valve.
