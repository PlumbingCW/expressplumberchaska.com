Emergency Leak Detection in Chaska, MN

If water is running where it should not, close the main shutoff valve first, then call. If the meter keeps spinning with every fixture off, the leak is hidden somewhere between the meter and the faucets. Finding it without tearing out drywall is what we do.

We locate leaks in walls, ceilings, floors, slabs, crawl spaces and buried lines in Chaska homes and businesses. Meter tests, pressure tests, listening equipment and thermal imaging keep repairs to the smallest possible area.

High Water Bill or Spinning Meter: Test It Yourself First

Turn off every faucet, the ice maker and the irrigation system, and make sure no one runs a toilet. Read the water meter, wait two hours with no water use, then read it again. If the numbers moved, water is leaking somewhere after the meter.

If the meter did not move, the leak may be intermittent. A toilet that refills every few hours is one example. The high bill may also come from use, not a leak. A dye tablet in each toilet tank, left for 15 minutes, catches silent toilet leaks.

Where Hidden Leaks Show Up

Ceiling or wall stain

A brown ring or bubbling paint often traces back to a supply line, a shower pan or a drain. Water can travel several feet from the source.

Hot spot on the floor

A warm patch under a slab or hardwood can mean a hot water line leak, especially in radiant heated floors.

Musty smell without a visible source

Mildew behind a wall or under a vanity signals slow moisture. Do not wait for the stain to appear.

Hissing in the walls

Pressurized water escaping a pipe makes a faint hiss when all fixtures are off.

Soggy lawn or low pressure

A wet patch in the yard or a drop in pressure at every tap suggests a service line leak outside.

Slab, Crawl Space and Buried Line Leaks

Most Chaska homes sit on basement foundations, so slab leaks are uncommon and mostly show up in additions, garages and some townhomes. When they occur, we locate the leak from above and recommend the least invasive repair, which can mean a reroute through the attic or walls rather than breaking the slab.

Under-house and crawl space leaks are easier to reach and often show as wet insulation or a damp dirt floor. Buried service line leaks between the meter and the house are traced by tracing the line and listening along its route, and they are repaired after a utility locate. What Leak Detection Costs

A leak detection visit is priced as a diagnostic service, often from about $200 to $500 in typical US ranges. The repair is priced separately once the leak is found. A job in a finished wall costs more to repair than an exposed basement pipe. What changes the price:

AccessExposed basement pipe is quick. A finished ceiling or a tile floor takes more care.
Number of zonesA single bathroom test is short. A whole house with several suspected zones takes longer.
Line typeCopper, PEX and galvanized pipe each fail differently and sound different under test.
Repair sizeA pinhole in a copper elbow is minor. A cracked buried main is a larger repair.

Many homeowners policies cover the resulting water damage from a sudden leak, and some cover the cost to access the pipe. Slow, long-term leaks are commonly excluded. Ask your insurer before work starts.

Meter Rules and Leak Alerts in Chaska

The City of Chaska recommends checking for a leak by reading the water meter before and after two hours of no water use. The City's metering system notifies customers if it detects water consumption for four consecutive days, which can flag a leak you had not noticed.

Utility Billing handles billing questions at 952-448-9200. Before any digging, Gopher State One Call at 651-454-0002 marks buried utilities. In summer, the City restricts non-essential irrigation from noon to 4 p.m. between May 1 and September 30, which is also a time when irrigation leaks appear as soggy patches.

Repairs after a leak is found follow the Minnesota Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and pressure tests are done before walls are closed.
